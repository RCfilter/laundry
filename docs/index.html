The three-product stack

This isn’t “use everything the internet recommends.” It’s a deliberate system for a new washer: clean well, avoid residue that sticks to the machine or fabric, and still handle body oils and gym wear without resorting to liquid sport detergent.

Main detergent 365 Unscented Powder Strong surfactants, enzymes, oxygen bleach — mud, dinginess, general cleaning.
Enzyme boost Dirty Labs Bio Enzyme Booster Extra lipase/protease/etc. for body oils and athletic wear — replaces the “liquid sport” role.
Rinse / feel Citric acid (food grade) Dissolved in water → softener drawer. pH balance and rinse aid — not fabric softener.
Not used: Liquid detergent (including sport liquids, pods with liquid fill, etc.)
Not used: Liquid fabric softener or dryer sheets with softening coating

Why no liquid detergent or fabric softener?

The goal with a brand-new machine is to avoid leaving physical and chemical “hooks” — anything that can accumulate in the drawer, gasket, pump, or fibers and give future soil, oils, or bacteria something to grab onto.

Fabric softener — avoid (strong agreement)

This part of the reasoning is well supported. Liquid fabric softener deposits a waxy, hydrophobic cationic coating on fabric. That coating:

  • Traps body oils and bacteria (especially bad on polyester gym wear)
  • Reduces absorbency on towels
  • Can contribute to drawer and gasket film in HE machines
  • Makes detergent less effective over time on coated fibers

Food-grade citric acid in the rinse is a different mechanism: it helps remove excess detergent and mineral film and balances pH. It improves feel without leaving a persistent fat-like coating. That’s why it goes in the softener compartment — same timing, different chemistry.

Liquid detergent — cautious agreement (with nuance)

Avoiding liquid on a new machine is a reasonable protective choice, not a universal law:

Concern (real)How powder + this stack addresses it
Drawer gunk — thickeners, fragrance carriers, and concentrated liquids can leave sticky residue in HE detergent compartments that low-water rinses don’t fully flush. Powder goes in the drum (or drawer only if your manual allows and it rinses clean). Nothing viscous sitting in the liquid cup.
Hard-water curds — some detergents react with calcium/magnesium and form insoluble scum (see jar test). 365 powder includes builders that help in hard water; citric acid in rinse helps strip mineral/detergent film.
Overdose residue — HE machines use little water; excess detergent of any form can remain. Powder + measured booster + extra rinse + citric acid = aggressive about leaving nothing behind.
“Need liquid for body oils” — liquids are often recommended for grease because they’re pre-dissolved. Dirty Labs booster adds lipase and other enzymes so you don’t need a liquid sport detergent for gym clothes.
Honest nuance Millions of people run HE machines on liquid detergent without problems. The issue is usually formula + dose + water hardness + drawer hygiene, not “liquid” as a category. Your powder-only approach trades convenience (can’t pour liquid on a stain in the drawer) for lower risk of sticky buildup on a machine you want to keep clean from day one. That’s a valid trade.

What “hooks” means in practice

  • Physical: undissolved granules, viscous films, waxy softener layers in gasket folds and dispenser channels
  • Chemical: soap scum (detergent + hard-water minerals), fatty-acid residues from incomplete oil removal, biofilm that grows on any of the above

Starting with a low-residue stack makes it easier to keep the machine “empty” so nothing old competes with each new wash.

What each product does

365 Unscented Powder

  • Surfactants — lift everyday dirt, body oils, and grease (the “carriers”)
  • Enzymes including lipase — start breaking down proteins and oils
  • Oxygen bleach — whitens, deodorizes organic residues; works best warm-to-hot
  • Builders — help in hard water; good on mud, dust, dinginess

Community starting dose: about ½ scoop per normal load (tune down if suds are heavy). Add to drum before clothes unless your manual specifies the drawer.

Dirty Labs Bio Enzyme Laundry Booster

  • Adds concentrated enzyme cleaning (lipase for body oils/sebum, protease for sweat proteins, etc.)
  • Fills the gap that liquid “sport” detergents usually cover — without introducing liquid to the machine
  • Effects on gym odor are cumulative; don’t expect one wash to fix a years-old funky polyester shirt

Follow package dose. Most useful on athletic wear, heavily soiled loads, and when washing in cooler water where enzymes still help but powder alone might leave more oil behind.

Food-grade citric acid (rinse)

  • Not a softener — doesn’t coat fibers with wax
  • Lowers rinse pH, helps flush excess detergent and mineral scale
  • Improves hand-feel on towels and cotton (fluffier, less “crunchy from alkali”)

How to use: Dissolve measured citric acid in room-temperature water (add acid to water, not the reverse). Pour into the softener compartment. Starting point: 1–2 teaspoons acid per load; increase slightly for very hard water.

Pre-dissolving is preferred for this system — it dispenses cleanly, avoids dry crystals in the drawer, and matches the “no mystery residue” goal.

How to run a load

General pattern for this stack on an HE front-loader:

  1. Add 365 powder and Dirty Labs booster to the drum before clothes (check booster label — some allow drawer).
  2. Load clothes. Don’t overload — enzymes and rinse need water contact.
  3. Warm or hot when fabric care allows (helps powder dissolve and oxygen bleach work).
  4. Pour pre-dissolved citric acid into the softener drawer.
  5. Select cycle (Normal, Towels, Sportswear, etc. per load type).
  6. Enable extra rinse (many enthusiasts use double extra rinse on a new machine).
  7. Remove promptly; leave door and drawer open to dry.

Lipase detergent list

Lipase is the enzyme that breaks down body oils and sebum — the main culprit in persistent gym-clothes odor. If you ever swap products, check whether a detergent actually contains lipase (many marketing claims are vague).

Gym clothes & odors

Polyester holds body oils more than cotton. A shirt can smell fine out of the dryer, then sour or “vomit-like” (butyric acid from bacteria on leftover oils) once your body heat warms it.

How this stack helps

  • Lipase (powder + booster) breaks oils into smaller pieces the wash can rinse away
  • No fabric softener — nothing trapping oils under a coating
  • Citric acid rinse — strips detergent/mineral film that can hold odor
  • Extra rinse — fewer enzyme breakdown products left behind

Still do these

  • Wash soon after sweating
  • Don’t overdose powder (residue traps odor)
  • Keep the washer gasket clean — biofilm can re-deposit on “clean” clothes
  • Expect improvement over multiple washes, not one miracle load

Protecting a new machine

The product choices above only work if the machine stays clean.

  • After every wash: wipe gasket; leave door and drawers open
  • Every load: clean lint trap (dryer)
  • Weekly: disinfect door seal
  • Monthly: tub clean cycle; remove and wash detergent drawer; citric acid or manufacturer tablets in drum
  • Periodically: drain pump filter (front loaders)
  • Annually: dryer vent duct cleaning

Run jar test on any new product before it enters the machine — gift detergents, pods, or “luxury” liquids included.

Reference: jar test & background chemistry

Optional deeper reading — useful when evaluating products outside this stack.

Jar test (detergent + your water)

  1. One cup tap water in a lidded jar + ½ tsp detergent
  2. Shake until maximally foamy; set aside
  3. When foam collapses, everything should dissolve back. Scum, curds, or flakes that won’t redissolve = too “soapy” for your water → machine buildup risk

Liquid vs powder (general)

Liquids excel at pre-treating oily stains; powders excel at mud, dust, and whitening with oxygen bleach. This site’s approach chooses powder + enzyme booster instead of liquid for machine-hygiene reasons, not because liquids never work.

Butyric acid / “vomit smell”

Usually residual body oils in synthetics + bacteria → volatile fatty acids. Not “detergent failed entirely” — often incomplete oil removal, washer biofilm, fabric softener coating, or poor rinse. This stack targets each of those.

What others do differently

Some enthusiasts pair 365 Sport liquid + 365 powder on every load. That works for them; this system deliberately replaces the liquid sport role with Dirty Labs booster to stay powder-only in the machine.

FAQ

Is avoiding all liquid detergent scientifically required?

No — but it’s a sensible risk-reduction strategy for a new HE machine, especially if you want zero liquid in the detergent drawer and you’re covering oils with lipase from powder + booster. The stronger hard rule is no fabric softener.

Why dissolve citric acid instead of putting powder in the softener drawer?

Pre-dissolved solution dispenses evenly, avoids crystals clogging the compartment, and fits the “nothing left behind” philosophy. Same chemistry, cleaner delivery.

Is 365 powder + Dirty Labs overkill?

There is overlap in enzymes. Booster is most justified for gym wear, heavy soil, and cool washes. For lightly worn delicates, skip or reduce booster.

Can I ever use liquid for stain pre-treat?

A small amount applied directly to a stain on the fabric (not through the machine drawer) is different from running liquid through the dispenser. Many people make that exception; it doesn’t require changing the whole system.
