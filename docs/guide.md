# Laundry Guide for Beginners

A practical introduction to detergents, enzymes, odors, and routines — for people new to laundry science or upgrading from "pods and forget."

> **One-sentence summary:** Use **liquid** for oils and grease; use **powder** for dirt, mud, dinginess, and stubborn odors. A good liquid alone works for most people; add powder for whites, towels, and heavy soil.

## Liquid vs powder

| Main problem | Best choice | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Oils, grease, food stains, body oils | **Liquid** (can pre-treat) | Everyday clothes, gym wear, food spills |
| Dirt, mud, dust, dinginess, whitening, odors | **Powder** (often has oxygen bleach) | Work clothes, gardening, white towels & sheets |

**Temperature:** Liquids work well in cold water. Modern powders are better in cold than old formulas, but ensure powder fully dissolves in very cold washes. Oxygen bleach works best warm-to-hot.

**Hard water:** Powders often excel (water-softening ingredients). Soft water: both usually fine.

## Ingredient cheat sheet

| Ingredient | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Surfactants** | Lift and carry soil; don't destroy stains chemically |
| **Protease** | Sweat proteins, blood, food proteins |
| **Lipase** | Body oils, sebum, grease — key for gym clothes |
| **Amylase** | Starch food stains |
| **DNase** | Oily human/animal residues (some sport detergents) |
| **Oxygen bleach** | Chemically bleaches organic stains; whitens; deodorizes; needs warm water + time |
| **Oxi (e.g. Biz)** | Boosts cleaning; softens water; may contain OBAs — great for whites, caution on darks |
| **Citric acid** | Rinse aid: balances pH, removes detergent/mineral film, improves feel |

**Oxi on colors:** Lighter than "light navy" → oxi with OBAs OK. Darker than "charcoal" → use dark-safe oxi without OBAs or skip.

**Citric acid dose:** Start 1–2 tsp in softener drawer; increase ~50% per 100 ppm hardness past ~150 ppm.

## Water, temperature & HE machines

- Use HE-labeled products in HE washers.
- **Dosage matters more than format:** too much liquid = residue; too much powder = undissolved granules. Most people overdose.
- Tune by watching suds: a light trace on the door glass after a few minutes is enough.

## Gym clothes & enzyme boosters

Gym odor is usually: trapped sebum in synthetics + sweat residue + bacteria.

**Enzyme boosters (e.g. Dirty Labs Bio Enzyme):** Help, especially lipase for body oils. Modest gain if detergent is already enzyme-rich; more noticeable with cold washes, persistent odors, or old polyester.

**Reality check:** Improvement is cumulative, not one-wash magic.

**Often bigger wins:**

- Correct dose (not too much)
- Wash soon after sweating
- Strong enzyme detergent
- No fabric softener on athletic wear
- Deep soak for bad items
- Clean the washer

**Polyester:** Holds oils more than cotton → "clean" out of dryer, sour when body heat releases volatile acids.

## The vomit smell (butyric acid)

Sour/vomit smell after washing = organic residue survived + bacteria/enzymes produced volatile fatty acids (butyric acid is detectable at tiny amounts).

**Causes:**

1. Body oils not fully removed (especially polyester)
2. Washer biofilm transferring to clothes
3. Enzyme breakdown products not rinsed away (overload, low dose, poor rinse)
4. Damp laundry sitting too long (anaerobic bacteria)
5. Fabric softener trapping oils under coating

**Fixes:** Adequate enzyme detergent, warm water when safe, no softener on gym wear, extra rinse, monthly washer clean, oxygen bleach on appropriate loads, don't leave wet laundry sitting.

## Example load-by-load routine

*From r/laundry — LG front loader, ~105 ppm hard water. 365 Sport + 365 Powder + Biz + citric acid. Double extra rinse on most loads.*

| Load | Biz | 365 Sport | 365 Powder | Citric acid | Temp | Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whites | ⅓ cup drum | 1 cap drawer | ½ scoop drawer | 2 tsp softener | Hot | Whites + 2× rinse |
| Light colors | ⅓ cup drum | 1 cap drawer | ½ scoop drawer | 2 tsp | Warm | Normal + 2× rinse |
| Darks/jeans | — | 1 cap drawer | ½ scoop drum | 2 tsp | Warm | Normal + 2× rinse |
| Bedding | ⅓ cup drum | 1 cap drawer | ½ scoop drum | 2 tsp | Hot | Bedding + 2× rinse |
| Towels | ⅓ cup drum | 1 cap drawer | ½ scoop drum | 2 tsp | Hot | Towels + 2× rinse |
| Sportswear | ⅓ cup drum | 1 cap drawer | ½ scoop drawer | 2 tsp | Warm | Sportswear + 2× rinse |
| Delicates | — | 1 cap drawer | — | 2 tsp | Cool | Delicates + 2× rinse |

**Why both Sport and Powder?** Sport = DNase + lipase for athletic soil; Powder = surfactants, more enzymes, oxygen bleach. Intentional overlap if you want max cleaning and aren't cost-limited.

## Machine care

**Washer:** Extra rinse when needed; monthly tub clean (tablets or citric acid); weekly gasket wipe; leave door/drawer open; monthly detergent drawer clean; periodic pump filter.

**Dryer:** Lint trap every load; vent ducts yearly; medium heat common default.

## FAQ

**Both Sport and powder every load — overkill?** Not if you want max cleaning and don't mind cost. Simplify to liquid-only for everyday/darks; add powder+oxi for whites, towels, heavy soil.

**Citric acid dry or dissolved?** Dry in softener drawer works on most HE machines. Dissolve in room-temp water if you see residue.

**Booster fix gym smell in one wash?** Rarely. Expect gradual improvement + dose/timing/softener/washer fixes.

**Fine in dryer, awful when exercising?** Residual oils in synthetics + body heat releasing odor molecules.
